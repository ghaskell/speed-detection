Detection Tuning

Number of raw speed measurements averaged into one smoothed reading. Lower values react faster to speed changes but are noisier (a single bad frame can spike the result). Higher values produce stable readings but take longer to reflect actual speed — a vehicle accelerating through the zone may appear slower than it really is. Default 3 is a good balance for 15 fps.

How many YOLO detections a vehicle needs before its speed is calculated. The tracker measures distance traveled between positions, so more positions = more accurate speed. Lower values catch fast vehicles that cross the zone quickly (e.g. 2-3 detections at 45 mph), but are less precise. Higher values are more accurate but may miss vehicles that pass through in just a few frames. Default 5 works well at ~15 fps for most traffic.

Only every Nth frame is sent through YOLO detection. At 15 fps, "2" means ~7.5 detections/sec — good for the i7-4790K. Setting to 1 processes every frame (highest accuracy, highest CPU load). Setting to 3+ reduces CPU usage significantly but gives YOLO fewer chances to see each vehicle, which can hurt tracking of fast-moving vehicles or cause track ID swaps. All frames still go into the capture buffer regardless of this setting.

A vehicle must exceed the speed limit for this many readings in a row (at any point during its track) to be classified as a speeder. Setting to 1 means a single over-limit reading is enough — catches everything but may flag momentary spikes from tracking jitter. Setting to 2-3 filters out single-frame noise and only flags vehicles that are consistently over the limit. This is the primary false-positive control.

Capture Sensitivity

After YOLO stops detecting a vehicle (it leaves the frame or gets occluded), the system waits this long before closing the track and making the speeder/non-speeder decision. Shorter values capture speeders sooner and free buffer memory faster. Longer values handle temporary occlusions (e.g. a vehicle hidden behind a tree for 1-2 seconds) without splitting into two tracks and losing speed history. Default 2.0s handles most brief occlusions.

When set to "Intersection Only", speeders are only captured if they passed through the defined intersection zone (configured on the Traffic Light page). Vehicles speeding outside the intersection are still counted in stats but won't be saved as speeder images. Requires an intersection zone to be configured.

Confidence Tier Multipliers

Controls how far over the limit a vehicle must be to trigger capture, based on how much tracking data we have. Vehicles with 3+ smoothed speed readings have reliable data, so "Full" can be set to 1.0x (any amount over the limit). Vehicles with only 1-2 readings (fast pass-throughs) have less certainty, so "Partial" defaults to 1.5x — they must be going 50% over the limit to be flagged. This prevents false positives from noisy short tracks while still catching genuinely fast vehicles.

Speeder Severity Thresholds

Once a vehicle is confirmed as a speeder (passes the tier multiplier gate above), these thresholds determine severity. Minor = at least this % over the limit. Major = at least this % over the limit. For example, at a 20 mph limit with Minor=0% and Major=25%: any speed over 20 is minor, over 25 is major. Saved images and dashboard stats are broken down by tier.
